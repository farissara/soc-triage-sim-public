1. Invitation to Take Part
You are being invited to take part in a research study being carried out as part of an MSc Cyber Security dissertation at the University of the West of England, Bristol (UWE Bristol). Before you decide whether to participate, it is important that you understand why the research is being done and what it will involve. Please take time to read the following information carefully and ask the researcher if anything is unclear.
2. What Is the Purpose of the Study?
The study investigates how cybersecurity analysts make decisions when working alongside AI-based decision-support tools. Specifically, it examines whether the level of confidence shown by an AI recommendation influences the analyst's willingness to verify alerts independently. The findings will contribute to academic understanding of human-AI teaming in Security Operations Centres (SOCs).
3. Why Have I Been Chosen?
You have been invited because you have an interest, background, or training in cybersecurity, IT, or a related discipline. The study is open to adults aged 18 and over. You do not need to be a professional analyst to take part; the simulation is designed to be accessible to participants across a range of experience levels.
4. Do I Have to Take Part?
No. Participation is entirely voluntary. You are free to decide not to take part, and you may withdraw at any point during the simulation simply by closing the browser tab — no data is recorded or submitted unless you complete the full session, at which point your anonymised results are submitted automatically to the secure research database. You do not need to give a reason for withdrawing, and there will be no penalty or disadvantage of any kind.
To keep the research data valid, each participant may only complete the simulation once per device/browser. Once you finish a session, this device will show a message confirming the study has already been completed if you try to start again.
5. What Will Happen If I Take Part?
If you agree to participate, you will be asked to:
  • Complete a short pre-task form: confirm informed consent and indicate your years of cybersecurity experience (None / <1 / 1–3 / 3+) and your familiarity with AI security tools (None / Some / Frequent). A Participant ID (e.g. P001) is assigned to you automatically — you never enter any personal details.
  • Triage a sequence of 20 simulated security alerts in a browser-based SOC dashboard. For each alert, you will see network and contextual details alongside an AI recommendation and confidence score, and you will choose either DISMISS or INVESTIGATE within a 20-second window.
  • Complete a short post-task questionnaire (six items in total: five Likert-scale items about your experience and one manipulation-check item).
  • View a structured debrief screen explaining the full purpose of the study, including the controlled element of deception (see Section 7).
The total time commitment is approximately 12 to 15 minutes.
6. What Are the Possible Disadvantages, Risks or Discomforts?
There are no anticipated physical risks. The simulation is designed to be realistic but not stressful. The 20-second per-alert timer is intended to mirror genuine SOC workload conditions; you are not penalised for missed deadlines, and your data remains valid regardless of the outcome.
Some participants may experience mild frustration when the post-task debrief reveals that some AI recommendations were deliberately misleading. This is a standard feature of decision-making research and is fully explained in the debrief. If you have any concerns afterwards, please contact the researcher.
7. Controlled Deception (Important)
For scientific reasons, this study includes a controlled element of methodological deception: the AI confidence scores you will see are artificially generated and randomly assigned to one of three experimental conditions, and a small number of the AI's "BENIGN" recommendations are deliberately incorrect. This design is necessary to test whether analysts over-rely on high-confidence AI recommendations.
The full details of the deception, including which condition you were assigned to and which alerts were misleading, will be revealed to you at the end of the simulation in the debrief screen. By giving consent, you confirm that you understand and accept this study design.
8. What Are the Possible Benefits?
There are no direct personal benefits. By taking part, you contribute to academic research into automation bias and analyst decision-making, which has implications for the safe deployment of AI tools in security operations.
9. Will My Participation Be Confidential?
Yes. The simulation is designed to be entirely anonymous. No directly identifying information is collected — your name, email, IP address, and device fingerprint are never collected in the research dataset. Your automatically assigned Participant ID is the only reference used, and demographic items are restricted to broad bands that cannot be used to identify you.
On completion, your anonymised results are transmitted over an encrypted (HTTPS/TLS) connection to a secure research database hosted in the United Kingdom, accessible only to the researcher. Downloaded data is then held on the researcher's password-protected, encrypted device, accessible to the researcher and dissertation supervisor only.
10. What Will Happen to the Data?
The anonymised dataset will be analysed for the dissertation report and may be cited in summary form in subsequent academic publications or conference presentations. Only aggregated statistics — never individual responses — will appear in any publication. Raw data files will be retained until completion of dissertation marking and the academic appeals window (12 months after submission, i.e. until approximately September 2027), after which they will be securely deleted in accordance with UWE Bristol Data Retention guidance.
Please refer to the accompanying Privacy Notice (below) for further detail on data handling and your rights under UK GDPR.
11. What If I Want to Withdraw?
You may withdraw from the simulation at any time before completion simply by closing the browser tab — no data is submitted in that case.
After completion, you may request that your submitted dataset be removed by emailing the researcher with your Participant ID. Withdrawal of submitted data is possible up to two weeks before the dissertation submission deadline (i.e. by mid-September 2026), after which data is irrevocably anonymised in aggregate analysis and individual records can no longer be located.
12. What Will Happen to the Results of the Study?
The results will be written up as part of the researcher's MSc dissertation in September 2026. A summary of findings can be made available to interested participants on request. The dissertation may also be deposited in the UWE Research Repository in line with university policy.
13. Who Has Reviewed This Study?
This study has been reviewed and approved by the supervisor (Ramya Hari) and the relevant UWE Bristol research-ethics review process (UFCE4B-60-M: Cyber Security Research Paper, Pre-Ethics Review Checklist).

2. What Data Is Collected?
The study deliberately avoids the collection of personally identifying information. The following categories of data are collected directly from you via the online simulation:
  • An automatically assigned Participant ID (e.g. "P001"). This is pseudonymous and not linked to your name or any external identifier.
  • Demographic items in broad bands: years of cybersecurity experience (None / <1 / 1–3 / 3+) and familiarity with AI security tools (None / Some / Frequent).
  • Behavioural data captured during the simulation: your decision (DISMISS / INVESTIGATE / TIMEOUT) for each of 20 alerts, response time per alert, mouse/trackpad hover dwell time on the AI panel and the alert-fields panel (not collected on touch devices, where a simple record of touch-input use is kept instead — see below), and your randomly assigned experimental condition (A, B, or C).
  • Post-task questionnaire responses: five Likert-scale items (1–5) and one manipulation-check item (1–5).
  • Session metadata: start and end timestamps (UTC); confirmation of consent with timestamp; confirmation that you scrolled through this Privacy Notice, with timestamp; and whether touch input was used during the session.
The following are not collected in the research dataset: name, email address, IP address, MAC address, browser fingerprint, device location, free-text comments, or any other information that could be used to identify you. As with any website, the page host (GitHub Pages) and the database provider (Supabase) transiently process IP addresses in routine security logs; these logs are never accessed or used by the researcher and form no part of the research data.
3. Why Is This Data Being Collected? (Purpose)
The data is collected solely for the purposes of academic research into analyst decision-making and automation bias in cybersecurity. The findings will be reported in the researcher's MSc dissertation and may be summarised in subsequent academic publications or conference presentations, in aggregated form only.
4. Lawful Basis for Processing
The lawful basis for processing your data is your CONSENT, given freely via the Consent Form/screen, in accordance with UK GDPR Article 6(1)(a). Because the data collected is pseudonymous and contains no special-category information, no additional Article 9 lawful basis is required. You may withdraw consent at any time (see Section 8).
5. Who Will Have Access to My Data?
Access to the raw dataset is restricted to: the lead researcher (Faris Sara), the dissertation supervisor (Ramya Hari), and UWE Bristol examiners and external markers (during marking only). The data is not shared with any third party, advertiser, commercial entity, or external organisation. No data is transferred outside the United Kingdom or the European Economic Area.
6. How Is My Data Stored and Protected?
  • During the simulation, data is held in your browser's local storage (localStorage); nothing is transmitted while the session is in progress.
  • On completion of the session, the anonymised dataset (containing no identifying fields — see Section 2) is transmitted over an encrypted (HTTPS/TLS) connection to a secure, access-controlled research database provided by Supabase (acting as data processor) on AWS infrastructure in London, United Kingdom. The database accepts submissions only — no data can be read back from the public application.
  • Exported files are stored on the researcher's password-protected, full-disk-encrypted laptop and on UWE OneDrive (encrypted in transit and at rest, hosted within the UK/EEA).
  • Files are accessed only via the researcher's UWE login credentials, which are protected by multi-factor authentication.
7. How Long Will My Data Be Retained?
Raw data files will be retained until 12 months after the dissertation submission deadline of September 2026 (i.e. until approximately September 2027), to cover the period of academic marking and any appeals process. After this period, the raw data files will be permanently and securely deleted. Aggregated, fully anonymised statistics may be retained indefinitely as part of the published dissertation.
8. Your Rights Under UK GDPR
Because data is held in pseudonymous form and only retrievable via your assigned Participant ID, you must include your Participant ID in any request for these rights to be exercised:
  • Right to be informed (this notice)
  • Right of access — request a copy of the data held about you
  • Right to rectification — request that inaccurate data be corrected
  • Right to erasure — request deletion of your submitted data, up to two weeks before the dissertation submission deadline (by mid-September 2026)
  • Right to restrict processing
  • Right to object
  • Right to withdraw consent (subject to the same mid-September 2026 cut-off)
  • Right to data portability (the JSON export already serves this purpose)
To exercise any of these rights, please email the lead researcher quoting your Participant ID: Faris2.sara@live.uwe.ac.uk, or contact the UWE Data Protection Officer at dataprotection@uwe.ac.uk.
9. Automated Decision-Making
The study presents simulated AI recommendations to participants as the research stimulus, but no automated decision-making is performed on participant data, and no profiling is carried out.
10. Right to Complain
If you are unhappy with how your data has been handled, you have the right to lodge a complaint with the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO): Wycliffe House, Water Lane, Wilmslow, Cheshire SK9 5AF — Helpline: 0303 123 1113 — https://ico.org.uk. You may also contact the UWE Bristol Research Ethics Committee at research.ethics@uwe.ac.uk if you have a complaint specifically about how the research has been conducted.

This Privacy Notice may be updated to reflect changes in study procedure or applicable law. The current version will always be available on request from the researcher.
